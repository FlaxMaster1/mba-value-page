The Value of a Wharton MBA

Everyone will have access to AI. A Wharton MBA builds what the tool can’t supply: the rigor to think a problem through, the judgment to make the call and answer for it, and a community that gives its time long after graduation.

Three pillars of a Wharton MBA

  • Invaluable Rigor

    Rigor is a necessary condition for getting value out of an MBA. The reps, the critical thinking, and the judgment built in uncomfortable situations are the muscles that speak for you in an interview or a promotion setting; the degree alone won’t. Transcript language

Invaluable Rigor

Rigor at Wharton isn’t difficulty for its own sake. It starts with a question faculty ask before putting anything in front of students: how is this going to help them after they leave? That focus on application and relevance runs through the MBA curriculum and the majors students choose to go deeper. Transcript language

Classrooms are built for discussion and group work, so you practice defending an analysis and communicating it well enough to get the resources it needs. Teaching draws on both academic research and faculty work with executives and companies, and what faculty learn there keeps returning to the classroom. Transcript language

Decision Making in the Age of AI

Michael Roberts uses AI seven to eight hours a day, and he says better models have made his judgment and domain expertise more important, not less. When he asks alumni and employers what they want in new hires, they ask for more than AI proficiency: using the technology responsibly, with good judgment and critical thinking, while staying accountable for the decisions that follow. Those are the abilities recruiters will test when they ask how you use AI. Transcript language

The work starts before the first class. In Pre-Term, students receive school-supported AI tools and attend a required session on how generative AI works, what it can do, and where it can go wrong. From there, they can pursue the Artificial Intelligence for Business major, take electives, and join AI-focused student organizations.

This fall, Roberts and faculty colleagues Ethan Mollick, Kartik Hosanagar, and Daniel Rock also led a workshop series showing students how they use AI in their own work. Transcript only: confirm before use

Community Above All Else

Wharton’s size gives you breadth, and its structure makes that size personal. Each class is divided into four Clusters and cohorts of about 70 students, and you meet your learning team at a two-day Pre-Term leadership retreat. From there, student-led clubs and conferences give you room to build something of your own.

The relationships continue long after graduation. Roberts recalls emailing an alum he had never met when he was a new assistant professor. A call was set up within 30 minutes, and the two ended up talking for two hours. He says students have the same access to alumni time and expertise every day.
